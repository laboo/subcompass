# The Unflattening

Oct 18, 2026 · @Aye Me Mine

Every calculation in the history of physics rests on one quiet decision: 1 × 1 = 1. It was a wise decision. Without it, numbers would have swollen beyond what any hand or machine could carry, and the great achievements of science could never have been made. But a choice made for convenience became a description of reality, and in that moment the universe was flattened. A one made of ones was declared identical to a single one, its parts forgotten, its depth pressed out like a flower in a book. We have studied that pressed flower ever since, with extraordinary skill, and wondered why it will not grow.

This work restores the depth. It keeps a different one at every level, a one that remembers what it is made of, and it asks for nothing more. There are two values, 1 and −1, which we write ⊕ and ⊖. The entire universe is built from these two numbers alone: signed binary. There are two ways to combine them: addition, which joins things within a dimension, and multiplication, which carries them into the next. There are containers, each holding the world of one dimension. That is the complete list. No forces are assumed, no constants smuggled in, no space presupposed. Everything that follows is arithmetic.

In the beginning there was a single point, the origin, holding a loose bag of ⊕ and ⊖, nothing yet bound. The Big Bang was not stuff exploding into space. It was that vast collection being factored, its members binding across dimensions, two points making a line, three making a plane, four making the solid world, each step leaving the same fingerprint: n dimensions, n + 1 points. Space did not contain the universe; space is what the universe became as it factored. And it happened at a real center, the origin, which we see everywhere at once only because we live inside a slice of what rose out of it.

That fingerprint is why nature keeps showing us triangles and tetrahedra in its deepest structures. They are not accidents of chemistry. They are the smallest units of the 2D and 3D worlds, showing through.

Anti-matter has long been treated as a second substance, a mirror-world ingredient that somehow went missing. It was never an ingredient at all. The anti-matter twin of anything is that same thing with every ⊕ and ⊖ flipped, a relationship rather than a stuff, and since everything is built from both values, the hunt for where it went becomes a question about arithmetic, which arithmetic can answer.

Order matters. ⊕ × ⊖ is not ⊖ × ⊕. Every bond carries a direction from the very first step, which means left and right were written into the universe before there was anything to be left or right of.

The pushes and pulls we call forces are not in the universe at all. They are what arithmetic looks like when it is flattened, seen from a world that perceives only results and never their parts. We will not ask anyone to take this on faith. We will show it, step by step, until the illusion falls away.

The same flattening explains the infinities that have haunted physics, the singularities at the hearts of black holes and the Big Bang, the endless terms that had to be cleared away by hand. They are not features of nature but the debris of forcing a layered world onto a single level, and so is the seamless continuum, which was only ever a stack of units pressed so flat their edges disappeared. Time, too, the dimension that has resisted every explanation, is what the next dimension feels like from inside the one below.

And the two great theories of the last century, each magnificent in its own realm, could never be joined because both were written in flattened arithmetic, read at different heights. Restore the levels and they become two floors of one building.

Two numbers, 1 and −1. Two ways to combine. Containers within containers. The universe was hiding inside the number one all along.
