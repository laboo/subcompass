I have an idea and am checking it before I write it out. **The idea.** Physicists once proposed (Harari's "rishon" model, 1979) that each electron, neutrino and quark is made of three smaller pieces of only two kinds: T, carrying ⅓ of the electron's charge, and V, carrying none, plus their antimatter twins T̄ and V̄. It turns out the baseline already contains these four pieces: they are its four ordered segments. [⊕ ⊕] is T (+⅓), [⊕ ⊖] is V (0), [⊖ ⊖] is T̄ (−⅓), [⊖ ⊕] is V̄ (0). Flipping every sign turns each piece into its twin, as the baseline says it should. A particle is three segments joined by their first points, like three sticks tied at one end. The baseline's existing annihilation rule then forces all three first points to have the same sign. So a particle can't mix matter and antimatter, and in Harari's model that ban had to be added as a separate rule. That leaves exactly 16 particles, one full family of the Standard Model: the electron and positron; the neutrino and antineutrino; the up and down quarks and their antiquarks. Each comes out with the right charge, read as plain integer arithmetic: 6 × charge = the sum of all six points. For example, up = TTV gives 2+2+0 = 4, and 4/6 = ⅔. **What makes it a new model.** The rules must change, not just how we read them. I'm adding one: empty space can lend a ⊕⊖ pair. That lets a lone down quark decay into up + electron + antineutrino, and charge is still conserved. I'll prove both in Lean. The model can't yet say how fast anything happens, and it has no quark colors or extra particle families. I'll write it up, build it on `team-0`, and check back within 5 minutes.
