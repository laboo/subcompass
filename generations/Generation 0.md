I have an idea and am checking it before I write it out. **The idea.** Physicists once proposed (Harari's "rishon" model, 1979) that each electron, neutrino and quark is made of three smaller pieces of only two kinds: T, carrying ⅓ of the electron's charge, and V, carrying none, plus their antimatter twins T̄ and V̄. It turns out the baseline already contains these four pieces: they are its four ordered segments. [⊕ ⊕] is T (+⅓), [⊕ ⊖] is V (0), [⊖ ⊖] is T̄ (−⅓), [⊖ ⊕] is V̄ (0). Flipping every sign turns each piece into its twin, as the baseline says it should. A particle is three segments joined by their first points, like three sticks tied at one end. The baseline's existing annihilation rule then forces all three first points to have the same sign. So a particle can't mix matter and antimatter, and in Harari's model that ban had to be added as a separate rule. That leaves exactly 16 particles, one full family of the Standard Model: the electron and positron; the neutrino and antineutrino; the up and down quarks and their antiquarks. Each comes out with the right charge, read as plain integer arithmetic: 6 × charge = the sum of all six points. For example, up = TTV gives 2+2+0 = 4, and 4/6 = ⅔. **What makes it a new model.** The rules must change, not just how we read them. I'm adding one: empty space can lend a ⊕⊖ pair. That lets a lone down quark decay into up + electron + antineutrino, and charge is still conserved. I'll prove both in Lean. The model can't yet say how fast anything happens, and it has no quark colors or extra particle families. I'll write it up, build it on `team-0`, and check back within 5 minutes.

Everything is committed locally and builds with no errors or gaps; nothing is pushed yet. **On `main`: `Subcompass/Suite.lean`.** **How a model plugs in.** A model fills in a short form: its kind of world and of event; what one event does; an empty starting world (the vacuum); two readouts: which Standard Model particles a world holds, and its total charge, in units the model picks. The template asks only for these, so it works whatever a model is built from. A model with no particles yet just reads none. **A shared particle list.** All 12 matter particles and the 6 force carriers and Higgs, with their measured charge, baryon number, lepton number and spin. Everything is an integer: charge is counted in thirds of the electron's. **What a test is.** A test is a claim about a model. Each team proves, in its own files, the tests its model passes, and can also prove the ones it fails; both are useful. "This can happen" tests are proved by listing the events, which Lean then runs. **Five tests to start:** charge conserved: no event makes or destroys charge; charge carried: all charge belongs to particles, none to loose leftovers; β decay: a down quark becomes an up quark, an electron and an antineutrino; annihilation: an electron and a positron destroy each other, leaving no matter behind; makes family n: every particle of family n can be made from the vacuum together with its antiparticle. **On `team-0`: Rishon's results** (`Subcompass/RishonSuite.lean`). It passes charge conserved, β decay, annihilation and family 1. It provably **fails** "charge carried". Making an electron from the vacuum leaves six ⊕ points loose; they carry charge but aren't a particle. That's a real flaw, and I've added it to `Rishon.md`. Families 2 and 3 are unproved. **Why it's built this way.** The tests are physics facts, so they don't favour any one way of building a model. A model that doesn't name particles can still prove "charge conserved". And because a team can prove a failure as well as a pass, the suite shows where each model breaks, not just where it works. **Suggestions for the other teams:** add tests for things that must *not* happen (for example, an electron turning into a neutrino, which would break charge conservation), and for a 3D world.
